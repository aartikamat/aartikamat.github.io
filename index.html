Arati Uday Kamat

Independent Researcher

Financial-systems risk and controls: auditing financial data and automated financial decisions · open datasets and preregistered studies

Previously about 16 years at IDBI Bank, India (2006–2022), Assistant Manager to Assistant General Manager (AGM from 2018).

Research focus. Automated rules that reject credit applications, payments, flagged transactions or trades are hard to evaluate, because outcomes for rejected cases are often missing or incompletely recorded (in credit scoring this is studied as reject inference). Public crypto markets offer a useful setting for studying part of this problem: a rejection can be recorded with the rule that fired, and the asset's later market path observed without executing a trade. Post-Rejection Follow-up Sampling (PRFS) is a documented follow-up procedure for doing this; it draws on about 16 years of banking operations and controls work. Limits: a rejected token's later price is not the return an executed trade would have earned, and it says nothing about how a rejected borrower would have repaid.

Working papers

Post-Rejection Follow-up Sampling: Measuring Outcomes of Rejected Decisions in Algorithmic DEX Trading

Kamat, A. U. (2026) · arXiv:2606.08228 · Dataset concept DOI 10.5281/zenodo.20043515

Introduces Post-Rejection Follow-up Sampling (PRFS), an observational methodology in which a separate tracking process samples the price and liquidity of each rejected token from the same live data path used by the rejecting system. The companion dataset contains 67,000 follow-up rows across 2,997 rejection events and 457 unique Solana token mints; in these data the deployed tracker sampled each tracked token every 10 minutes and kept one open record per token at a time. The reported estimand is the observed post-rejection forward return; no causal claim about a hypothetical accepted trade is made.

Hour-Aware Adaptive Risk Management for Autonomous Memecoin Trading on Solana DEXs: Evidence, Theory, and Design Lessons from a 15-Day Deployment

Kamat, A. U. (2026) · arXiv:2606.08232 · SSRN abstract 6564803 · Manuscript concept DOI 10.5281/zenodo.19670718 · Dataset concept DOI 10.5281/zenodo.20043301

Reports a 15-day paper-traded deployment of an autonomous memecoin trading system on Solana decentralised exchanges (190 trades, 29 March to 12 April 2026). A test of three exploratorily selected weak entry hours against all other hours is directional and not confirmatory (Mann-Whitney p = 0.56), and removing the top three trades (1.6 percent of the sample) makes the cumulative return unprofitable. The contribution is measurement infrastructure, a deposited assertion-based reproduction script, and three design lessons for small-sample trading studies.

Outcome-Classified Precision Auditing of Filter Rules in Algorithmic DEX Trading: Evidence from 2,400 Rejection Events

Kamat, A. U. (2026) · arXiv:2607.02830 · SSRN abstract 6638259 · Manuscript concept DOI 10.5281/zenodo.19720040 · Dataset concept DOI 10.5281/zenodo.19987695

Audits eight filter rules in a DEX trading system's filter stack, using about 2,400 rejection events from a two-week window in April 2026. It counts rejected events whose tracked price later fell to half or less of its first tracked value within 24 hours ("saves") or rose to double or more ("misses"); the save-to-miss ratio is descriptive and not by itself evidence of filter skill.

RED-2400: A Public Benchmark of Algorithmically-Rejected Trading Events with Outcome Labels

Kamat, A. U. (2026) · arXiv 2605.12151 [q-fin.TR] · SSRN abstract 6702198 · Multi-platform deposit: Zenodo + Kaggle + IEEE DataPort

Public benchmark dataset of algorithmically rejected DEX trading events from a live Solana filter stack: 6,660 rejection events, 169,123 forward-outcome observations and 1,837 lifecycle-tracker snapshots over the window 2026-04-10 to 2026-05-02 UTC. Event-level outcome linkage is available for about half of the events (3,333 of 6,660 exact matches); mint-level coverage is near-complete (1,075 of 1,076 mints). CC-BY-4.0 licensed. Deposited across six platforms (arXiv, SSRN, Zenodo, Kaggle, IEEE DataPort, Hugging Face). RED-2400 is a dataset name, not a count, and is the first window in a planned dataset series.

Auditing Collector-Generated Graduation Labels on Pump.fun: Measurement Error and Temporal Non-Generalization

Kamat, A. U. (2026) · arXiv:2607.02823 · Companion dataset RED-PUMP-2026-v1 · Dataset concept DOI 10.5281/zenodo.20633486 (always resolves to the latest version, currently v1.6 at 10.5281/zenodo.23018519)

Audit, under a pre-specified analysis protocol, of the graduation labels written by an off-chain data collector for 749,816 pump.fun token launches (12 May to 10 June 2026). The recorded label is the collector's own classification of what it saw while polling a feed of the 50 newest tokens, not the platform's graduation event, and its "timeout" label does not establish non-graduation. A locked logistic association model discriminates well in the 15-day development period (AUROC 0.859) but does not generalise to the following 14 days (AUROC 0.464; 95 percent interval 0.411 to 0.520, which includes 0.5). The dataset's documentation notes that this failure mainly reflects a calendar-spline term in the frozen model rather than evidence that the market changed, and that the last day of the validation cohort is outcome-selected.

Measurement artefacts in public crypto market data: A concurrent audit of oracle, exchange, lending, derivatives and bridge feeds

Kamat, A. U. (2026) · SSRN abstract 7062579 · Five companion datasets on Zenodo: RED-ORACLE, RED-ARB, RED-LIQ, RED-BASIS, RED-BRIDGE

Asks how far descriptive regularities in data collected from public crypto market interfaces reflect the collection instrument rather than the market. It audits five concurrent feeds spanning 57 days in 2026 (Pyth oracle prices against an OKX benchmark, Solana decentralized-exchange quotes, Aave V3 liquidations and utilization, OKX basis and funding, and Wormhole bridge messages) and derives an audit protocol for data users. The five feeds are deposited as open datasets on Zenodo under CC-BY-4.0: RED-ORACLE-2026, RED-ARB-2026, RED-LIQ-2026, RED-BASIS-2026 and RED-BRIDGE-2026.

Do Two On-Chain Observation Pipelines See the Same Tokens? Cross-Pipeline Coverage on the Solana pump.fun Launchpad

Kamat, A. U. (2026) · arXiv:2609.18975 · Reproducibility package concept DOI 10.5281/zenodo.21399918

Compares the output mint sets of two separately configured observation pipelines from one research programme on the Solana pump.fun launchpad: a cohort-detection pipeline and a rejection-filtering pipeline. In the June-July 2026 window, where the rejection stream covers the whole 17.5-day window, the cohort set contains 623 mints and the rejection set 1,742, with 5 overlapping mints (0.803% of cohort; 0.287% of rejection). In the earlier June window a collection gap leaves the rejection stream covering only 4.4% of the cohort window, so its overlap count is too small to be informative. Only raw counts, observed proportions and Jaccard/Dice indices are reported. The two pipelines' outputs were nearly disjoint, consistent with their different selection rules; the paper proposes reporting cross-collector overlap and collector coverage as a routine check.

RED-REJECT-2026-v2: A 96-Day Public Corpus of Algorithmic Filter Rejections with Post-Rejection Follow-up Samples on the Solana pump.fun Ecosystem (current release v2.3)

Kamat, A. U. (2026) · Dataset concept DOI 10.5281/zenodo.21402476 · 716,762 sample records across 4,635 unique SPL token addresses

Public benchmark corpus of algorithmic filter rejections with post-rejection follow-up samples on the Solana pump.fun ecosystem, 2026-04-11 to 2026-07-16. The 96-day window contains one collection gap (no new rejections from 3 June and no samples from 4 June until 24 June 2026), leaving about 75.6 days of sampling coverage. Corpus size: 716,762 sample records across 4,635 unique SPL token mint addresses and 18,237 distinct rejection events under eight named filter families (shares of sample rows: downtrend_24h 56.13 percent, marketcap_out_of_range 21.38 percent, multi_tf_fade 11.98 percent, timeSlot_not_strong 5.17 percent, hour_blacklist 2.36 percent, ultra_fast_reject_exit 1.64 percent, utc_hour_blocked 0.92 percent, h6_sellers_dominating 0.41 percent).
