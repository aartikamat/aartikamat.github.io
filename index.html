Hour-Aware Adaptive Risk Management for Autonomous Memecoin Trading on Solana DEXs: Evidence, Theory, and Design Lessons from a 15-Day Deployment

Kamat, A. U. (2026) · arXiv:2606.08232 · SSRN abstract 6564803 · Manuscript concept DOI 10.5281/zenodo.19670718 · Dataset concept DOI 10.5281/zenodo.20043301 · Earlier versions were titled "A Multi-Layer Intelligence Framework"

Reports a 15-day paper-traded deployment of an autonomous memecoin trading system on Solana decentralised exchanges (190 trades, 29 March to 12 April 2026). A test of three exploratorily selected weak entry hours against all other hours is directional and not confirmatory (Mann-Whitney p = 0.56), and removing the top three trades (1.6 percent of the sample) makes the cumulative return unprofitable. The contribution is measurement infrastructure, a deposited assertion-based reproduction script, and three design lessons for small-sample trading studies.

Post-Rejection Follow-up Sampling: Measuring Outcomes of Rejected Decisions in Algorithmic DEX Trading

Kamat, A. U. (2026) · arXiv:2606.08228 (v2, 2026-09-17) · SSRN abstract 6607301 · Manuscript concept DOI 10.5281/zenodo.19671656 · Dataset concept DOI 10.5281/zenodo.20043515 · Earlier versions (SSRN, Zenodo) were titled "A Methodology for Counterfactual Outcome Measurement in Algorithmic DEX Trading"

Introduces Post-Rejection Follow-up Sampling (PRFS), an observational methodology in which a separate tracking process samples the price and liquidity of each rejected token from the same live data path used by the rejecting system. The companion dataset contains 67,000 follow-up rows across 2,997 rejection events and 457 unique mints on one Solana sub-venue. The reported estimand is the observed post-rejection forward return; no causal claim about a hypothetical accepted trade is made.

Correction in preparation: in the deployed data, the tracker sampled each tracked token at a fixed 10-minute cadence and kept one record per token, so the follow-up schedule and horizon described in the posted versions were not realised. A corrected version is in preparation.

Outcome-Classified Precision Auditing of Filter Rules in Algorithmic DEX Trading: Evidence from 2,400 Rejection Events

Kamat, A. U. (2026) · arXiv:2607.02830 · SSRN abstract 6638259 · Manuscript concept DOI 10.5281/zenodo.19720040 · Dataset concept DOI 10.5281/zenodo.19987695 · Posted versions superseded

Audits eight filter rules in a DEX trading system's filter stack by comparing the losing trades each filter avoided ("saves") with the winning trades it blocked ("misses"), using about 2,400 rejection events from a two-week window in April 2026.

Superseded: the posted versions (arXiv, SSRN and Zenodo) are superseded. Their wider headline ratio counted a token's quick disappearance from the price feed as a save; that outcome tier is withdrawn, because in these data follow-up usually ended at the token's next rejection rather than at token death. A rebuilt analysis, which compares the filters' saves and misses with what a fair game (prices with no predictable drift) would produce and replicates the comparison on later data, is in preparation.

RED-2400: A Public Benchmark of Algorithmically-Rejected Trading Events with Outcome Labels

Kamat, A. U. (2026) · arXiv 2605.12151 [q-fin.TR] · SSRN abstract 6702198 · Multi-platform deposit: Zenodo + Kaggle + IEEE DataPort

Public benchmark dataset of algorithmically rejected DEX trading events from a live Solana filter stack: 6,660 rejection events, 169,123 forward-outcome observations and 1,837 lifecycle-tracker snapshots over the window 2026-04-10 to 2026-05-02 UTC. Event-level outcome linkage is available for about half of the events (3,333 of 6,660 exact matches); mint-level coverage is near-complete (1,075 of 1,076 mints). CC-BY-4.0 licensed. Deposited across six platforms (arXiv, SSRN, Zenodo, Kaggle, IEEE DataPort, Hugging Face). RED-2400 is a dataset name, not a count, and is the first window in a planned dataset series.

Correction: the preprint's comparison of tokens that left the price feed soon after rejection with other rejected tokens (Section V) is withdrawn. In these data, follow-up for a rejection event usually ends at the token's next rejection, not at token death, and the lifecycle tracker's "gone" state is not a verified token death. The Zenodo dataset record (version 4.0, September 2026) carries the corrected documentation; the data files are unchanged. A corrected version of the paper is in preparation.

Auditing Collector-Generated Graduation Labels on Pump.fun: Measurement Error and Temporal Non-Generalization

Kamat, A. U. (2026) · arXiv:2607.02823 (v4, 2026-09-10) · SSRN abstract 6915560 (earlier version) · Dataset concept DOI 10.5281/zenodo.20633486 (always resolves to the latest version, currently v1.6 at 10.5281/zenodo.23018519) · Companion dataset RED-PUMP-2026-v1 · Earlier versions were titled "Pump.fun Graduation Regime Windows"

Audit, under a pre-specified analysis protocol, of the graduation labels written by an off-chain data collector for 749,816 pump.fun token launches (12 May to 10 June 2026). The recorded label is the collector's own classification of what it saw while polling a top-50 feed, not the platform's graduation event, and its "timeout" label does not establish non-graduation. A locked logistic association model discriminates well in the 15-day development period (AUROC 0.859) but does not generalise to the following 14 days (AUROC 0.464; 95 percent interval 0.411 to 0.520, which includes 0.5). The negative result is reported as specified, with a reproducible correction history.

Correction notice (28 September 2026, dataset v1.6): the validation failure mainly reflects a calendar-spline term in the frozen model, not evidence that the market or the feature–label relationship changed; the analysis protocol, frozen after earlier analyses of the same data, is a pre-specified re-analysis protocol rather than a pre-registration; and the last day of the validation cohort is outcome-selected. Corrected versions of the paper are in preparation.

Correction (September 2026): the current arXiv version (v4) retracts the survival-analysis estimates of the earlier versions, including the pooled 0.198 percent graduation rate, the "3.18x decline" built on it, the Telegram "lift" figures and the wallet-intent narrative, because each treated the collector's label as the platform's graduation event. The earlier arXiv versions (v1 to v3) and the SSRN posting still show those claims and should not be relied upon.

A Multi-Venue Solana/DeFi Microstructure Data Corpus: The RED-2400 Family v2

Kamat, A. U. (2026) · arXiv:2610.00005 · SSRN abstract 7062579 · Corpus dataset paper covering five deposited datasets: RED-ORACLE, RED-ARB, RED-LIQ, RED-BASIS, RED-BRIDGE

Corpus-level dataset paper describing the RED-2400 family v2 of Solana / DeFi microstructure benchmarks. Five deposited datasets on Zenodo under CC-BY-4.0: RED-ORACLE-2026 (Pyth Network prices and publication lags for BTC, ETH and SOL with OKX benchmark prices), RED-ARB-2026 (328,186 rows of Jupiter sell quotes against OKX prices for six Solana tokens), RED-LIQ-2026 (Aave V3 Ethereum liquidations, 3,547 distinct events in 18,750 rows, plus a six-chain lending-utilization panel), RED-BASIS-2026 (OKX spot-perpetual basis and funding observations for BTC, ETH and SOL), RED-BRIDGE-2026 (360,714 Wormhole cross-chain messages). All five cover a common window of about 57 days (2026-05-08 to 2026-07-05). Standardized schema conventions, cross-dataset lineage, and reproducibility manifests for external researchers extending the methodology.

Correction (September 2026): version 1.1 of each dataset (28 September 2026) corrects the documentation; the data files are unchanged. Descriptions in the posted paper that differ from the version 1.1 records (for example, liquidation counts, the number of chains in the utilization panel, and DEX "venue" labels, which are the first leg of an aggregator route) should be read as corrected there.

Do Two On-Chain Observation Pipelines See the Same Tokens? Cross-Pipeline Coverage on the Solana pump.fun Launchpad

Kamat, A. U. (2026) · arXiv:2609.18975 · SSRN abstract 7128818 · Reproducibility package concept DOI 10.5281/zenodo.21399918

Compares the output mint sets of two separately configured observation pipelines from one research programme on the Solana pump.fun launchpad: a cohort-detection pipeline and a rejection-filtering pipeline. In the June-July 2026 window, where the rejection stream covers the whole 17.5-day window, the cohort set contains 623 mints and the rejection set 1,742, with 5 overlapping mints (0.803% of cohort; 0.287% of rejection). In the earlier June window a collection gap leaves the rejection stream covering only 4.4% of the cohort window, so its overlap count is too small to be informative. Only raw counts, observed proportions and Jaccard/Dice indices are reported. Collector configuration, not only market behaviour, shapes which tokens an on-chain study observes; the paper proposes reporting cross-collector overlap and collector coverage as a routine check.

Public benchmark corpus of algorithmic filter rejections with post-rejection follow-up samples on the Solana pump.fun ecosystem, 2026-04-11 to 2026-07-16. Dev-Wallet Silence Is Not a Rug Predictor on Solana Pump.fun: Empirical Evidence from a Positive-Class and Control-Panel Study

Kamat, A. U. (2026) · Concept DOI 10.5281/zenodo.21434629 (always resolves to the latest version) · Corrected v1.3 (2026-08-26) · Small-sample study; labels are data-collector classifications

Small-sample empirical study of whether transaction silence (6 hours or more) of the wallet that a data collector labelled as a token's dev wallet, in the 24 hours before an operationally classified rug event, distinguishes those Solana Pump.fun tokens from an eligibility-filtered control panel. Both the dev-wallet label and the rug classification are the collector's own operational labels, not verified on-chain facts, so the results describe these labels rather than token creators or confirmed rugs. RED-2400 Replication Toolkit: A Python Package for Reproducible Filter-Precision Auditing

Python toolkit accompanying RED-2400. Packages the filter-audit workflow as an installable library (source on GitHub; not published on PyPI). Release v2 attaches outcomes to each rejection event.
